In the old city, where the pols fold into each other like memories,
there is a lane so narrow that two cycles cannot pass without apology.
That is where the cassette shop stood.

A blue board with hand-painted letters. Half the paint gone. Below it,
a door that never fully opened, and inside, walls lined with music.

He was never on time, but he was always there. Glasses sliding down
his nose. A pencil tucked behind his ear. He didn't just sell cassettes.
He introduced people to songs they didn't know they needed.

## I. THE LANE
The lane carried everything—spices, pressure cookers, school bags,
gossip, and film songs leaking from open windows.

Somewhere between a sweet shop and a tailoring unit, the blue board
appeared like a pause in the noise.

Inside, the air was warm and tilted—like time slowed down in that room
and refused to catch up.

## II. THE MAN
He knew what you were going through, even if you didn't say it.
Heartbreak? He had a side for that.
Lonely nights? Another shelf.
Exam stress? Instrumentals.
First love? He would smile, without looking up, and slide out a cassette
with a faded cover and say, "This one."
