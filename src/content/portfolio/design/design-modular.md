> **“The future is smooth. Just not for you.”**

_The Road to Somewhere_ is a satirical poster created as part of my **Colour Theory** module, using colour, contrast and visual storytelling to turn an everyday frustration into a larger commentary on development and political promises.

The poster imagines a road where the promised future and the everyday reality exist side by side: a smooth, decorated path reserved for the VIP, while everyone else navigates potholes, traffic and chaos.

## **The Idea**

The starting point was something extremely ordinary: **Indian roads and potholes.**

Potholes are so familiar that they have almost become part of the landscape. They are complained about, photographed, joked about and then encountered the next day again.

I wanted to turn that familiarity into something more exaggerated.

The central visual idea was to create a strange contrast between a perfectly smooth, almost ceremonial road and the broken roads surrounding it. The central car moves confidently towards a road marked **“FINISH”**, while everyone else is forced to navigate the rougher paths around it.

The result is deliberately humorous, but the humour comes from something very real.

***

## **The Visual Approach**

The poster uses an illustrated, almost editorial-cartoon language rather than a realistic depiction of a road.

The composition is built around a strong central perspective. The smooth road leads directly into the distance, creating a sense of progress and optimism, while the potholes on either side interrupt that idea.

The characters, vehicles, flags and exaggerated expressions add another layer of satire. Everything looks almost celebratory at first glance, but the closer you look, the more contradictory the scene becomes.

The typography reinforces this contrast.

**“TIME PASS IN INDIA”** establishes the tone immediately, while **“THE FUTURE IS SMOOTH / JUST NOT FOR YOU”** turns the visual joke into the central argument of the poster.

> **“The joke is in the picture. The colour just makes it harder to ignore.”**

***

## **Colour**

Since this project was developed for my **Colour Theory** module, colour was not treated simply as decoration.

I wanted the palette to feel warm, nostalgic and almost cheerful: the kind of colour language you might associate with an old illustrated newspaper, travel poster or political campaign.

That warmth creates an interesting contradiction with the subject.

The earthy oranges, yellows and creams make the scene feel inviting, while the darker outlines and contrasting colours help separate the characters, vehicles and road from one another.

The Indian tricolour appears within the environment as another visual cue, connecting the scene to the larger idea of nation-building and development.

The colour palette therefore works on two levels: **it makes the poster visually appealing while quietly supporting its satire.**

***

## **Composition & Typography**

The composition was designed to make the viewer enter the image from the top and gradually travel down the road.

The central perspective creates a visual hierarchy:

**Title → Road → Characters → Potholes → Finish**

The typography follows the same exaggerated tone as the illustration. Large serif and display lettering gives the poster the feeling of an old newspaper or periodical, while the smaller editorial text underneath expands the joke into a written commentary.

The combination of illustration, headline, subheading and body copy allows the work to sit somewhere between a **poster, newspaper cover and satirical editorial page.**

***

## **The Poster**

![Poster on Potholes on Indian Road](/uploads/pothole-poster.jpg)

The final composition brings together the colour study, illustration, typography and written commentary into one deliberately overloaded visual scene.

Rather than hiding the message inside the design, I wanted the contradiction to be immediately visible: **one perfect road, surrounded by the reality everyone else has to deal with.**

***

## **What I Took From It**

This project made me realise that colour theory becomes much more interesting when it is connected to **meaning rather than just colour combinations**.

The same warm, celebratory palette could have been used to make a completely positive image. Here, it creates a tension with the subject and makes the satire feel lighter without making the underlying issue disappear.

For me, the project became less about designing a poster _about potholes_ and more about understanding how **colour, composition, illustration and typography can work together to make an ordinary subject say something larger.**
